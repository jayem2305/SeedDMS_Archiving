Identity Authentication Verifying one's identity online can be challenging. It is not a guarantee that someone is who they claim to be just because they say so. This is where authentication comes in. Authentication ensures that an individual is truly who they claim to be by requiring unique, verifiable credentials that are difficult to forge or replicate. Identity authentication involves validating and confirming an individual's identity using specific credentials such as biometric characteristics (e.g., facial recognition, fingerprints, iris scans), government-issued identification documents (e.g., passports, national IDs, driver’s licenses), or secure password-based systems. Biometric authentication, in particular, enhances security by relying on unique physical or behavioral traits that cannot easily be replicated. Document- based authentication verifies legitimacy by cross-checking official records, while password-based authentication grants access only to users who provide a correct combination of credentials. By integrating these methods, identity authentication strengthens security measures, minimizing unauthorized access and ensuring that only legitimate users can interact with sensitive systems or services. A study conducted by (Vandana & Kaur, 2022), entitled "A Study of Biometric Identification and Verification System", talks about the disadvantages of using passwords and PINs for user identification, as they are more prone to theft and misuse. Biometric security systems employ physiological and behavioural traits to verify identity. These systems use voice, fingerprint, iris, and image recognition techniques. To identify the most efficient strategy, the study analyzes unimodal and multimodal biometric systems and highlights the significance of authentication in information security. It summarizes biometric recognition systems, including information on their functionality and potential problems. The integration of various biometric attributes, as well as the potential, economic significance, and prospects for biometric technology, are also covered in this article. The article made by (Gunuganti, 2023) explores the concept of continuous authentication through behavioral biometrics as a response to the limitations of static authentication methods, such as passwords and PINs. This study highlights the importance of continuously validating user identity during active sessions, enhancing security against impersonation or unauthorized access. Behavioral biometrics, including keystroke dynamics and mouse movements, are utilized to monitor user behavior in real time, ensuring seamless protection without disrupting user convenience. The research emphasizes that continuous authentication offers improved data security, particularly for sensitive systems, while addressing vulnerabilities inherent in traditional methods. Moreover, it identifies the necessity of advancing biometric algorithms to enhance adaptability and protection in evolving cybersecurity landscapes. (Zukarnain, Muneer, & Aziz, 2022) examine various authentication methods for securing mobile identities, highlighting the growing importance of protecting sensitive information stored on smartphones. The study identifies vulnerabilities in traditional methods, such as passwords and SMS authentication, and emphasizes the adoption of advanced techniques like fingerprint identification, face recognition, and identity-based public key cryptography (ID-based PKC). Additionally, it introduces multi factor authentication approaches, including user trip routes and geolocation-based methods, to enhance security with minimal user intervention. A notable contribution of the study is the implementation of asymmetric encryption protocols using user inputs as encryption keys, eliminating the need for costly certificate authorities.
